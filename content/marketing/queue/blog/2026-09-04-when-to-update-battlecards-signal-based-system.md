# When Should You Update Your Battlecards? A Signal-Based System

Most teams update their battlecards on a schedule. Quarterly review. Maybe a big rewrite after a conference where you see your competitors presenting.

That's the wrong way to do it.

Competitors don't change on your review schedule. They change when they need to — mid-quarter, mid-sales-cycle, sometimes mid-deal. A quarterly review means you're always working with stale intel.

Here's what I do instead: update battlecards based on signals, not calendar dates.

---

## The core problem with scheduled updates

I talked to a PM at a 40-person SaaS company last year. They had a dedicated person doing quarterly CI reports. Really thorough stuff.

But their battlecards were still getting their reps killed in deals. Why?

The report came out in January. In February, their main competitor killed their free tier, added a "Teams" plan, and repositioned their homepage from "analytics tool" to "decision intelligence platform." By March, reps were still using the January battlecard.

Three months of stale intel. Every rep walking into demos with the wrong competitive positioning.

The gap isn't effort. It's timing.

---

## What actually triggers a battlecard update

There are five change types worth tracking. In rough order of how fast you need to react:

### 1. Pricing changes (update within 24 hours)

Anything on their pricing page. New tiers, removed tiers, price increases, free tier modifications, annual discount changes.

This is the one that will get your reps caught off guard in real-time. A prospect who already saw the new price is going to ask questions you're not prepared for.

24-hour rule. Non-negotiable.

### 2. Free-tier or trial changes (update within 48 hours)

This is subtler than pricing but matters just as much for top-of-funnel positioning. If a competitor kills their free tier, that's displacement opportunity — former free users are suddenly evaluating paid options.

If they add a free tier and you don't have one, you now have an objection you weren't handling before.

### 3. Feature additions or removals (update within a week)

Homepage hero copy, features page, comparison tables. Anything that changes how they describe what they do.

New feature callouts matter, but deletions matter more. When they remove a feature from the homepage, it usually means it wasn't landing — or they're repositioning away from it. Either way, there's an angle there.

### 4. Messaging and positioning shifts (update within two weeks)

This is the subtlest signal. The homepage headline changes. The "who it's for" section gets rewritten. The ICP language shifts from "growth-stage startups" to "mid-market teams."

These changes are slow to show up in deals, but when they do, your battlecard will be off in a way that's hard to diagnose. "Why isn't this resonating?" often traces back to your positioning being three months behind theirs.

### 5. Hiring signals (flag, don't update immediately)

Two enterprise AE postings means they're going upmarket. A VP of Marketing hire means messaging overhaul is coming. A senior ML engineer hired in January means an AI feature announcement in Q2.

Don't rewrite the battlecard yet — flag it and schedule a review for 60-90 days out when the product changes materialize.

## A practical trigger system

Here's the actual system. Not fancy. Just what works.

**Daily:** Automated monitoring of pricing page, features page, homepage. Any change triggers a review.

**Weekly:** Scan their job listings and note any AE/product/marketing hires. Log them somewhere.

**Monthly:** Review the flagged hiring signals from the past 30 days and add anticipated changes to the battlecard as a "watch" section.

**Quarterly:** Full review of messaging, comparison positioning, and overall competitive landscape. The only scheduled update.

The daily automated piece is what makes this work. You can't manually check 4-5 pages per competitor every day — not reliably, not without it becoming a job. So you automate it and only spend time on actual changes.

## What to actually change in the battlecard

Not every detected change needs a full rewrite. Here's how to triage:

**Pricing change** → Update the pricing section immediately. Add a note if the change happened recently ("as of [month], they [changed X]"). Reps need to know this is fresh intel.

**Free tier killed** → Add to "objections" section: "They used to have a free tier and killed it. Here's how to use that." Add to "displacement opportunities" if relevant.

**Feature added they didn't have before** → Check if it's a gap you had. If yes, either own it ("we handle this differently via X") or wait for your product roadmap answer. Don't hide it.

**Feature removed** → This is often an angle. Why did they remove it? If it was a differentiator they used to push, there's a story there.

**Messaging shift** → Update the "how they position themselves" section. This is what reps read to understand the prospect's frame of reference.

## FAQ

**How often should you review battlecards?**

As often as your competitors make significant changes. For most teams, that means pricing and features weekly (or when a change is detected), and a full messaging review quarterly. The mistake is treating all battlecard updates as a quarterly ritual.

**What pages should you monitor per competitor?**

Pricing page, main features/product page, homepage (especially the hero section), any /compare or /alternatives pages they have, and their careers page for hiring signals. Five pages per competitor covers most of what matters.

**What if you have 15 competitors?**

Pick your top 5 by deal presence (the ones who show up most often in competitive deals) and monitor those closely. The other 10 can be quarterly-reviewed. Not all competitors deserve the same attention.

**Do you need a CI analyst for this?**

No. Automated monitoring + a PM or sales enablement person who does 30 minutes of triage per week covers it for most teams under 50 people. The analyst layer adds value at scale, but it's not required to maintain current battlecards.

**What's the fastest way to get started?**

Monitor the pricing page. Just that. Start there, see one change, and watch how fast you update the battlecard when you actually catch something. That converts more teams than any pitch deck I've ever written.
