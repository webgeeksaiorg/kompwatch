Monday morning used to take 3 hours.

Not because I had a lot to do. Because I had 12 competitor tabs pinned and I was manually reading through pricing pages, changelogs, and feature lists to see what changed.

Every. Single. Monday.

The thing I kept missing: changes that happened on Wednesday. Or Saturday at 2am when the product team pushed an update. By Monday, I was already behind — and in competitive deals, "already behind" means losing without knowing why.

So I built the thing I wanted to exist. A tool that watches competitor pages automatically, detects changes, and emails me a plain-English summary. Not a dashboard I have to log into. Not a 40-feature platform requiring a dedicated CI manager. Just: "here's what changed, here's why it matters."

Here's what I've learned tracking competitors this way for 8 months:

1. **Pricing pages lie**. The number changes but the annual discount footnote is where the real movement happens.

2. **The changelog is the best signal most PMs ignore**. Competitors announce features there before anywhere else.

3. **Quiet deletions are louder than additions**. When a competitor removes their free tier mention or buries their pricing, something's shifting.

4. **Weekend changes are real**. A lot of pricing tests go live Friday afternoon. Manual Monday checks miss them entirely.

5. **Job postings aren't just hiring signals — they're roadmap signals**. Three ML engineer listings means something's coming in 6 months.

Still building this. Still 0 paid subscribers. But the product works and the insight above is genuine.

What signals do you track for competitors that most people miss?
