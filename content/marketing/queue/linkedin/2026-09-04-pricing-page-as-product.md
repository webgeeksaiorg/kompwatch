**A pricing page is a product. Most companies don't treat it that way.**

I've been watching how SaaS companies update their pricing pages for a while now. Some patterns I keep seeing:

**The "hide the price" move:** Enterprise tiers go dark (contact us). Usually means they're testing whether buyers will self-qualify at a higher price point. Sometimes signals they lost deals on price and want more control.

**The mid-cycle add:** New features get quietly added to existing tier descriptions without a price change. This is either a genuine bet ("let's see if this increases conversions") or they're under competitive pressure and trying to match a competitor without admitting it.

**The downgrade:** Features that were in lower tiers get bumped to higher tiers. Almost always demand-side pricing pressure — they found that the cohort using those features converts to paid at a higher rate.

**The testimonial swap:** The social proof changes. New industry verticals get highlighted. Often a tell that they're pivoting ICP.

None of these are secrets. They're all visible to anyone who looks. The problem is remembering to look — and distinguishing changes that matter from cosmetic noise.

That's the actual problem to solve: continuous signal, not occasional reconnaissance.
