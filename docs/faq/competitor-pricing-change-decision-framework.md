# What Should I Do When a Competitor Changes Their Pricing?

**Short answer:** Don't panic, and don't immediately copy their move. Work through four steps: understand who the change is for, read what actually changed (not just the number), wait 48 hours before acting, then decide if you need to respond — and how.

---

## Step 1: Don't Assume It's an Attack

The instinct when a competitor cuts prices is to treat it as a threat. Sometimes that's right. Often it isn't.

Competitor pricing changes happen for reasons that have nothing to do with you:

- They raised a round and can subsidize growth temporarily
- They need to hit an ARR logo count before a fundraise or board meeting
- They had churn from a price-sensitive segment and are patching it
- Their user research showed their pricing was confusing
- A new CEO or VP of Product decided to simplify

A competitor that drops prices after a Series B isn't necessarily targeting your customers — they may be going after a different segment entirely.

**The first question to ask is: who is this price change for?**

---

## Step 2: Read What Actually Changed, Not Just the Number

"Competitor cut prices 30%" is a headline. It's not a strategy. Open the change in KompWatch and read the diff.

What to look for:

**Which plan changed?**
- Entry plan: targeting small customers or PLG motion
- Mid-market tier: different conversation
- Enterprise tier restructure: largely irrelevant unless you're selling enterprise

**What happened to limits?**
A price cut sometimes comes with tighter feature or seat limits. $79 → $49/mo sounds like a win for the market until you notice the seat limit dropped from 5 to 2. The price went down but so did the value.

**Did the free tier change?**
Free tier moves are the most strategic pricing signal:
- Free tier added → they're going bottom-up, betting on PLG expansion
- Free tier killed or capped harder → their PLG motion failed; moving sales-led
- Free tier limits tightened → trying to push free users to paid

**Does the page feel different beyond the numbers?**
Sometimes a price change is the visible part of a repositioning. The copy shifts, the ICP in the testimonials changes, the comparison table gets updated. The price change is the symptom; the repositioning is the strategy.

---

## Step 3: Wait 48 Hours Before Doing Anything

The worst competitive response is a knee-jerk pricing change made the afternoon you see the update.

Reasons to wait:

1. **It might be a test.** Competitors A/B test pricing pages. The change you saw might revert in 72 hours once they see conversion data.
2. **You need time to think strategically.** "They went lower, so we should too" is rarely the right answer. Usually it's better to hold your price and sharpen your differentiation.
3. **You need to check your data.** Are you actually competing against this company in deals? How often do prospects mention them? Is this company even in your ICP?

Use the 48 hours to:
- Check your CRM for deals where this competitor is mentioned
- Ask your last three AEs how often this competitor comes up
- Review the diff more carefully for anything you missed on first read
- Talk to one current customer about whether their pricing perception matters

---

## Step 4: Decide — Respond, Monitor, or Ignore

After 48 hours, you have three options:

### Respond (rare)

Warranted if:
- The change directly attacks your ICP (they launched a tier priced at exactly your ICP's budget)
- A price decrease will show up in live demos before you can address it with differentiation
- Multiple current customers are likely to see it and raise it on renewal

Response options: adjust your own pricing, update battlecard talking points, brief your sales team, add a comparison to your `/pricing` or `/vs/them` page.

### Monitor (most common)

Warranted for most pricing changes. You've noted what changed, you understand the likely intent, and you'll watch for follow-up signals (more changes, messaging shifts, job postings confirming a new direction).

Set a KompWatch alert on their pricing page at High severity if you haven't already.

### Ignore (occasionally right)

Warranted if the change is on a plan you don't compete on, in a segment you don't sell to, or if it's clearly cosmetic (reformatted page, no actual price or feature change).

---

## Common Mistakes

**Copying the price cut.** Lowers your margin without fixing the underlying issue. If they're subsidizing growth via VC money, you can't win a price war.

**Overreacting to a test.** Wait to confirm the change is real and sustained before briefing your team.

**Under-reacting to a free tier launch.** A new free tier is one of the most disruptive competitive moves. If a competitor just went freemium, it changes the comparison your prospects make. Address it in your messaging within 48 hours.

**Not telling your sales team.** Even if you decide not to change your pricing, your reps need to know what changed so they're not blindsided mid-demo.
