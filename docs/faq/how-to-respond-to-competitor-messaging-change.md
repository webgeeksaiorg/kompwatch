# How to Respond When a Competitor Changes Their Messaging or Positioning

**Short answer:** When KompWatch alerts you to a competitor messaging or positioning change, run this four-step playbook — verify, brief, update, monitor. Most teams that miss the window do so because they see the alert and open a tab to "look at it later." Structure the response the moment the alert lands.

---

## Step 1: Verify (15 minutes)

Before doing anything else, confirm the change is real and durable.

1. Click through to the KompWatch diff view and confirm the wording change.
2. Open the competitor's live page yourself — do you see the new copy?
3. Check whether the change is on one page or multiple: homepage, product page, meta titles, OpenGraph tags. A coordinated multi-page change is a real repositioning. A single-page change is probably a test.
4. Check the KompWatch snapshot history: did the same page change and revert in the previous 30 days? If yes, this is likely an A/B test variant, not a strategy shift — flag it for awareness but don't respond yet.

**If it's isolated and/or has reverted before:** Log it in your competitive notes and watch for 2 more cycles before taking action.

**If it's coordinated and persistent:** Proceed to Step 2 immediately.

---

## Step 2: Brief your team (same day, max 48 hours)

A competitor's positioning change affects multiple functions:

| Team | What they need to know | Why it's urgent |
|---|---|---|
| **Sales / SDR** | New competitor framing they'll hear in objections | Prospects who just visited the competitor's site will use the new language in your next call |
| **Content / SEO** | The category terms the competitor is claiming | If they're creating content around a new term, you need to be ranking for that term too |
| **Paid / Demand Gen** | The new value prop in competitor ads (check ad libraries) | If competitor updates their homepage copy, their Google Ads headline is usually next |
| **Product Marketing** | Full context for battlecard update | PMM owns the response; everyone else needs the briefing to hold until PMM has reviewed |

**Quickest format:** A one-paragraph Slack message to #competitive-intel with: (1) what changed, (2) which competitor, (3) what you think it signals, (4) what each team should watch for. Attach the KompWatch diff link.

---

## Step 3: Update your competitive artifacts (within 48 hours)

**Battlecard:** Update the "How they position" and "Their messaging vs. ours" sections. If their new positioning directly attacks your differentiation, update the "Handle this objection" section with counter-messaging.

**Positioning doc / messaging house:** If the competitor has moved into a category term you also use, flag it. This usually means either (a) the category is validating and you should double down, or (b) they're trying to blur the distinction and you need to sharpen it.

**One-pager / competitive brief:** If you have a short competitive brief for your sales team, update the "how they pitch" section.

---

## Step 4: Watch for the follow-through (30–60 days)

A real strategic repositioning is always followed by supporting signals:

- **New blog content** targeting the new category terms (usually within 2–4 weeks)
- **New paid ads** using the new hero copy (usually within 2–3 weeks — check their Meta Ad Library and Google Ads Transparency Center)
- **New case studies** from the ICPs they're now targeting
- **New landing pages** for new use cases or personas

If none of these follow-through signals appear within 60 days, the repositioning either stalled internally or was a test that didn't get approved. You can downgrade its threat level.

If the follow-through signals arrive, the repositioning is real and you have a 60-day window to respond with your own content and messaging before the new framing sticks in the market.

---

## Common mistakes

**Overreacting to a single page change.** One hero rewrite is not a strategy shift. Wait for multi-page coordination before responding publicly.

**Updating your battlecard but not briefing sales.** A battlecard update that lives in a Notion doc is worthless. The briefing to your active sales reps is the action that matters.

**Waiting for "the official announcement."** By the time they send a press release, the repositioning is 4–8 weeks old and your sales team has already faced the new framing in demos.

**Updating your positioning to mirror theirs.** The correct response to a competitor claiming a category is usually to sharpen your differentiation from that category, not to follow them into it.
