# What's the Difference Between a Competitive Analysis and a Battlecard?

Both are competitive intelligence documents — but they serve different purposes, different audiences, and are consumed in different contexts.

---

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| | **Competitive Analysis** | **Battlecard** |
|---|---|---|
| **Length** | 1–2 pages per competitor | 1 page per competitor (often half-page) |
| **Audience** | PMM, product, leadership, sales enablement | Individual sales reps |
| **When it's used** | Reference, planning, quarterly reviews | Live sales calls, objection handling |
| **What it covers** | Full picture — snapshot, pricing, ICP, strengths, weaknesses, win/loss patterns, objections, one-liner | Compressed essentials — pricing, top 3 objections + responses, one-liner |
| **Update cadence** | When something material changes (triggered by monitoring alerts) | Same — must match the analysis |
| **Shelf life** | 30–60 days without monitoring; indefinite with automated alerts | Same as analysis |

---

## The Mental Model

Think of the **competitive analysis** as the full research doc. It's the source of truth that gets updated by the person who owns competitive intelligence (PMM, RevOps, a founder, whoever).

Think of the **battlecard** as the compressed export of that research — formatted for a rep who has 10 seconds to glance at something mid-call.

> The analysis is what you maintain. The battlecard is what you hand to sales.

Both need to stay current, which is why the update trigger matters more than the format.

---

## What Goes in Each

### Competitive Analysis (9 sections)
1. Competitor snapshot (what they do, stage, funding)
2. Pricing tiers with verified dates
3. ICP + positioning (who they target and how)
4. Strengths (scored honestly)
5. Weaknesses (specific, not generic)
6. Win / loss patterns (from actual deals)
7. Top 3 objection responses
8. One-line summary (under 20 words)
9. Change log (dated history of updates)

### Battlecard (condensed)
- Elevator pitch (1–2 sentences)
- Pricing comparison (us vs them, current)
- Top 3 objections + responses (the "objection cheat sheet")
- When to lead with us, when to be cautious
- One-liner (same as analysis section 8)

## Which One Should I Build First?

**Build the analysis first**, then export a battlecard from it.

If you write a battlecard without an underlying analysis, it's easy to make it one-sided, stale, or inconsistent across competitors. The analysis forces you to do the research honestly (including listing competitor strengths). The battlecard is then a quick compression exercise.

## How KompWatch Helps Keep Both Current

KompWatch monitors each competitor's pricing page, features page, and homepage. When any of those change:

1. You get a digest with a plain-English summary of what changed
2. You review the relevant section in your **analysis** and update it (usually 5 minutes)
3. You update your **battlecard** to match (usually 2 minutes)
4. You log the update in the analysis change log so your team sees it's being maintained

This is what makes the difference between a living document and a 6-month-stale Notion page.
