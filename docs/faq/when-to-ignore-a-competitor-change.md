# When to Ignore a Competitor Change (And When to Act Immediately)

**Short answer:** Most competitor changes are noise. A pricing change or tier restructure warrants action within 24 hours. A hero image swap or blog post can wait — or be ignored entirely. Use the four-category triage below.

---

## The Four Categories

### 1. Act within 24 hours

These changes can affect live sales conversations before you know about them.

| Change | Why it's urgent |
|---|---|
| Pricing page: price dropped, tier removed, free tier added/killed | Prospects may have already seen it; your reps may be selling against stale numbers |
| "Contact us" replaces a listed price | Moving upmarket — affects your ICP overlap immediately |
| New plan targeting your exact customer segment | Direct move into your space |
| Feature added that eliminates your key differentiator | Your sales team needs updated talking points now |
| Competitor acquisition or shutdown announced | Prospects will ask about it |

See [Responding to a Major Competitor Move](./responding-to-a-major-competitor-move.md) for the 24-hour checklist.

### 2. Note it and review weekly

Worth knowing, not worth dropping everything for.

- Feature announcements (unless they're directly competitive with your core value prop)
- Messaging or hero copy changes (repositioning signal — interesting, not urgent)
- New case studies or testimonials (tells you who they're selling to)
- Changelog entries (track the pattern, not the individual entry)
- Job postings (leading signal for 3–6 month horizon; not actionable today)

### 3. Read once, then discard

- Blog posts and content marketing (unless it's a direct "/vs/you" comparison)
- Social media announcements (useful only if they announce something before their site updates)
- Award badges or review widgets added to the homepage

### 4. Ignore

These are almost never competitive intelligence. They're A/B testing, cosmetic work, or compliance housekeeping.

- Hero image or stock photo change
- Footer link updates
- Layout or CSS changes with no copy change
- New pricing FAQ entries without any actual price change (objection handling content)
- Cookie consent banner updates
- Team page edits

---

## Why This Framework Matters

When KompWatch first flags a change, it arrives with a severity rating (Low / Medium / High / Critical) assigned by AI. That rating is your first filter. But the triage above gives you the judgment layer — because even a "Medium" change on a pricing page means something different than a "Medium" change on a blog post.

The goal is to make the habit sustainable. If every change triggers a response, you'll stop responding to any of them. Save your attention for the ones that actually affect your sales motion.
