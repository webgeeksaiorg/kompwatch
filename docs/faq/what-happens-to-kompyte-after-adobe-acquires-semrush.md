# What Happens to Kompyte After Adobe Acquires Semrush?

Adobe completed its $1.9 billion acquisition of Semrush in February 2026. Kompyte — the competitive intelligence tool bundled into Semrush plans — is now three acquisitions deep inside Adobe's enterprise marketing suite. Here's what that means for teams that rely on Kompyte for day-to-day competitor tracking.

## What Actually Changed When Adobe Bought Semrush?

Semrush acquired Kompyte in 2022 to add a CI layer to its SEO platform. When Adobe acquired Semrush in February 2026, the entire Semrush portfolio — including Kompyte — transferred to Adobe. The Kompyte product team now reports into an organization whose core business is creative software and enterprise marketing cloud contracts, not competitive intelligence tooling for SaaS teams.

## Will Kompyte Still Work After the Acquisition?

In the short term, yes — product continuity through an acquisition is standard practice. The realistic risk isn't immediate shutdown; it's gradual strategic deprioritization. The pattern in enterprise SaaS acquisitions:

| Timeframe | Typical pattern |
|---|---|
| 0–12 months | "Business as usual" — existing features maintained, roadmap slows |
| 12–24 months | Pricing realignment — self-serve tiers shrink or disappear; seat costs increase |
| 24–36 months | Product consolidation — Kompyte bundled deeper into Adobe Experience Cloud, accessible only via enterprise contracts |

The question isn't whether Kompyte will keep running next month. It's whether the self-serve, SMB-friendly version of Kompyte will still exist when your Semrush plan comes up for renewal.

## Will I Still Be Able to Access Kompyte Through My Semrush Plan?

That depends on which Semrush plan you have and how Adobe structures renewals going forward. As of May 2026:

- Existing Semrush subscribers with Kompyte access retain it through their current contract term.
- Adobe's go-to-market model is annual enterprise contracts at five- to six-figure price points — not $99/mo self-serve SEO tools.
- Mid-market and SMB customers are the segment most likely to see Kompyte access either priced out or bundled into an enterprise tier they can't justify.

If you're evaluating ahead of your Semrush renewal, this is the right time to run a parallel CI tool test — before you're forced to decide under contract pressure.

## What Kompyte Features Am I Most Likely to Lose?

The features most at risk when a CI tool is absorbed into an enterprise marketing suite:

- **Self-serve setup** — enterprise suites require onboarding sessions and sales handoffs
- **CSS selector / targeted page monitoring** — niche technical features that serve power users, not enterprise marketing teams
- **Low-cost tiers** — $99–$499/mo plans tend to get consolidated into enterprise bundles
- **Independent roadmap** — feature velocity slows when product decisions go through multiple approval layers
- **Transparent pricing** — Adobe's pricing model is "contact sales," not a public pricing page

## What Can I Do With My Existing Kompyte Data?

Before your Semrush contract changes, export everything you can:

1. **Change history and battlecard content** — download or screenshot any CI reports you've built in Kompyte.
2. **Competitor URL lists** — export your monitored domains so you can replicate monitoring in a new tool.
3. **Digest history** — save any AI summaries or change logs you reference in sales calls or internal docs.

KompWatch cannot import Kompyte data directly, so manual export is the only path to preserving your history before you switch.
