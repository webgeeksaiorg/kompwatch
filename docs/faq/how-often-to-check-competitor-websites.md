# How Often Should I Check Competitor Websites for Changes?

**Short answer:** Automate it so the question doesn't apply — but if you're picking a monitoring frequency, use **every 6 hours** for competitors in active deals, **daily** for market-context players, and **weekly** for the long tail.

---

## The Rule: Frequency Should Match Deal Exposure

The right monitoring cadence depends on how much a competitor affects your pipeline right now:

| Competitor type | Recommended cadence | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Primary competitors (in your deals) | Every 6 hours | Pricing changes can flip a deal within hours of being published |
| Adjacent competitors (market context) | Daily | You want trend-level awareness, not same-day alerts |
| Long-tail / emerging players | Weekly | Enough to catch repositioning before it becomes a pattern |
| Competitors you track for research | Weekly or manual | Low signal-to-noise; no urgency |

---

## What Happens If You Check Too Infrequently

- A competitor drops pricing on Monday. You find out Friday from a prospect who switched.
- A competitor launches a new feature. You learn about it in a lost-deal review two weeks later.
- A competitor repositions from "for marketers" to "for RevOps." You miss the shift until their ads start appearing in your pipeline.

Same-day or next-day awareness is the goal for primary competitors. Everything else can lag.

---

## What Happens If You Check Too Frequently

Monitoring every hour (or real-time) on a large competitor set generates noise:
- Minor copy edits, timestamp updates, and A/B test rotations fire alerts
- Alert fatigue sets in and your team stops reading digests
- You spend time triaging non-changes instead of acting on real ones

The sweet spot for most PMMs is **every 6 hours** — close enough to catch same-day moves without noise.

## The Real Answer: Stop Checking Manually

The question "how often should I check?" assumes manual checking. The actual answer is: **don't check — automate it.**

Manual checking always drifts toward never. A bookmark folder of competitor sites visited weekly turns into monthly visits, then quarterly, then "I'll look when something comes up in a deal."

Automated monitoring means the answer is always "the last time our cron ran" — not "the last time I remembered to open a browser tab."
