# How Does KompWatch Pair with Gong, Chorus, or Clozd?

**Short answer:** Very cleanly — they don't overlap. Gong/Chorus capture what was said in the sales call. Clozd captures what the buyer says in retrospect. KompWatch captures what the competitor's site actually showed during the evaluation. Together they form a complete win/loss picture.

---

## The three data streams in a full-stack win/loss program

Every deal has three data streams you want to understand:

| Tool | What it captures | When you get the data |
|---|---|---|
| **KompWatch** | What the competitor's site actually said while the deal was live | Automatically, continuously |
| **Gong / Chorus** | What was said in the sales call — buyer language, competitor mentions, rep messaging | After each call |
| **Clozd / Wynter** | What the buyer says in retrospect about why they chose or rejected you | After the deal closes |

None of these replace the others. They answer different questions.

---

## What each layer reveals

### KompWatch (competitor-side artifact)
- Did the competitor drop their price 11 days before this deal closed?
- Did they ship a feature directly addressing your prospect's objection?
- Did they change their homepage hero to directly target your ICP while this deal was evaluating?

This is the objective, timestamped record of what the competitor was doing — not what anyone thought they were doing.

### Gong / Chorus (sales conversation)
- How did the competitive objection actually come up in the call?
- What did the rep say when the prospect mentioned the competitor?
- Was the competitor mentioned early (buyer-driven evaluation) or late (rep handling objection)?

This is the recorded reality of the conversation, not a rep's post-close recollection.

### Clozd / Wynter / buyer interviews (buyer retrospective)
- What does the buyer say they valued about the competitor?
- What was the stated reason for not choosing you?
- How does the buyer describe the evaluation in their own words, 2–4 weeks after the decision?

This is the buyer's interpretation — shaped by their memory and framing — but often the most candid feedback you'll get.

---

## Why they need each other

**Gong/Chorus without KompWatch:** You know the rep handled the pricing objection poorly, but you don't know if the competitor had actually just dropped their price (which changes the correct fix dramatically).

**Clozd without KompWatch:** You know the buyer said "they offered better integrations," but you don't know if that was already true 6 months ago or if the competitor shipped those integrations 3 weeks before your deal closed.

**KompWatch without Gong or Clozd:** You know the competitor shipped a new feature — but you don't know if it came up in the call, whether your rep addressed it, or how the buyer weighted it.

The combination turns vague deal retrospectives into precise, falsifiable data.

---

## The practical workflow

1. **During the deal:** KompWatch is running in the background, snapshotting the competitor's key pages every 1–6 hours.

2. **At deal close:** Check KompWatch's change history for the 30–60 days before close. Flag any HIGH-severity changes that correlate with the deal timeline.

3. **In the win/loss debrief:** Bring the KompWatch artifact to the Gong call review. "Did this pricing change come up in call 3?" Suddenly the conversation is anchored in facts, not impressions.

4. **In the buyer interview (Clozd):** Use KompWatch context to probe deeper. If you know the competitor dropped a tier, you can ask the buyer: "Were you aware their entry pricing changed during the evaluation?"
