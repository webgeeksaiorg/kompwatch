# Who Owns Competitive Battlecards?

Short answer: **whoever has the closest view of why deals are won and lost** — usually Product Marketing (PMM) or a dedicated Competitive Intelligence (CI) analyst. At smaller companies, that's often the founder or head of sales.

---

## Ownership by Company Stage

| Stage | Typical battlecard owner |
|-------|--------------------------|
| Seed / early-stage | Founder or Head of Sales |
| Series A–B | Head of Marketing or first PMM hire |
| Growth (50+ employees) | Product Marketing Manager |
| Enterprise | Dedicated CI team, sometimes embedded in Sales Enablement |

There's no single right answer. The owner just needs two things: **access to competitive intel** and **editorial authority** to decide what goes in the card.

---

## The Real Bottleneck Isn't Ownership

The bottleneck is always the same regardless of who owns the battlecards: **there's no reliable system for knowing when competitors change something important**.

Checking 5–10 competitor websites manually every week isn't scalable. Most battlecard owners fall into a reactive pattern — they update after a rep loses a deal and mentions it in Slack, not before.

KompWatch removes that bottleneck. The monitoring runs automatically, and change alerts land in your inbox (daily or weekly digest depending on your plan). The owner keeps full editorial control; the intel-gathering is automated.

## How Ownership Typically Works with KompWatch

1. **PMM / CI owner** sets up competitor tracking (URL + optional CSS selector for key sections like pricing)
2. **KompWatch** runs daily snapshots and surfaces changes with AI-generated summaries
3. **Owner reviews** the weekly digest, decides which changes are battlecard-worthy
4. **Owner updates** the relevant battlecard section (takes minutes, not hours, because the AI summary is already formatted for competitive context)
5. **Sales team** is notified via Slack or the digest email that the battlecard was updated

## What if No One Owns It Today?

Start small. Assign one person — even informally — and give them KompWatch's free plan (2 competitors, weekly digest). Once they see a competitor change surface in their inbox before a rep asks "did you see what [competitor] did?" — the value of formal ownership becomes obvious quickly.
